# 4 · User personas

Verbatim from `raw files/User Personas.txt`.

## Persona 1 — "The Resolution Repeater" *(primary)*

**Maya, 24, junior marketing associate.** She's downloaded four habit trackers, and each
died within three weeks — she'd skip a day, feel guilty, then avoid the app entirely. She
already uses BeReal-style apps with her friend group and shares gym progress in a group
chat, which is the behavior we're productizing. Her goal is going to the gym 4×/week and
reading before bed; her frustration is that *"nobody knows when I quit, so quitting is
free."*

**Design implication:** the product must make skipping **visible to friends without making
it humiliating** — supportive accountability, not shame.

## Persona 2 — "The Accountability Buddy Pair" *(primary)*

**Dan and Chris, 29, long-distance friends** who already text each other "did you run
today?" They don't want a big social network; they want a **private two-person pact with
proof**, because they both know a text saying "yep, ran" is easy to fake.

**Design implication:** the app must work brilliantly at **group size 2**, not just 12 —
most real accountability happens in pairs. Approval from one specific person carries more
weight than likes from many.

## Persona 3 — "The Group Challenge Organizer" *(secondary)*

**Priya, 31,** runs a "75 Hard"-style challenge with seven coworkers every January. She
currently manages it through a spreadsheet and a group chat full of unverifiable claims.
She's our **monetization target**: she'll pay for a group plan, custom rules, and challenge
templates, and she recruits 7 users every time she starts a challenge.

**Design implication:** group creation and invite flow must be **frictionless** (invite
codes like Setlog), and she needs light **"admin" powers** like setting the daily window.
